Sine function

Modeling demand is usually based on elasticity and cross elasticities. We usually have a time series with for price quantity for products, (we might have income, advertising, and competitor prices time series as well), and we want to estimate the elasticities.

  • So some really basic questions are given this data:
    1. Can we fit a demand function and
    2. Can we infer self and cross elasticities.
    3. If we have an elasticity what is the error and for what prices range is it valid
    4. Is there a different a long term elasticity.
    5. If demand is periodic does that mean I should also have a periodic elasticity?

We end up pondering these questions for a number of reasons. We collect data but it often lacks support in terms of different price changes. Few or no price changes create numerical as well as deeper identification issues.

E.g. just because the retailer next door is closed for a week, does not mean the one two doors down the road is now a monopolist and can set prices without regard to competition. we can mange to survive a week one way or another. The same is true if there is a stock-out for one product, for a week or for half a day. Half a day of stock-out together with a price reduction (to clear the product) does not mean we have a solid data for demand at that reduced price. But it can lead to having a bunch if zeros in our data and that Q is very sensitive to how we sample it. Also the store closes so if we decide to sample by the hour we should indicate that these entries have no weight. We also have people returning product giving us possible negative demand. Another issues we are likely to see is a price decrease together with a reduced demand. That gives a nonsensical demand curves and likely to populate our elasticity refression with high leverage points that mess up our estimates. A Models which has contrainsts like homogeneity, symmetry, and negativity will issue errors.

If we have price/quantity time series for related products, say complements Would it make more sense to infer the mean and covariance matrix to model the demands using a Cholesky decomposition for the covariance to minimize parameters, and then estimate the elasticities from the mean + covariance data?

  1. Suppose we assume that supply is more or less fixed (i.e., supply side responds much more slowly than demand, prices >> cost so that P determines Q in our market.

Identification of elasticities from mean/covariance

There is an identification problem and here is an explanation

From \((\mu,\Sigma)\) of quantities alone we can’t identify elasticities when prices barely move.

Let us try to understand why we would fail:

  • Let
    • \(y_t=\log Q_t\in\mathbb{R}^n\)
    • \(x_t=\log P_t\in\mathbb{R}^n\) with exogenous prices1 and

\[ y_t=\alpha + Bx_t+\varepsilon_t,\quad \varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\Omega). \]

  • Since this is a \(log(P)\) \(log(Q)\) model, \(B\) is the elasticity matrix, i.e., \(B_{ij}\) is the elasticity of product \(i\) with respect to product \(j\). With self price elasticity \(B_{ii}\) and cross price elasticity \(B_{ij}\) for \(i\neq j\).

In reduced form,

\[ \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}(y_t)}_{\Sigma_y}=B\,\underbrace{\mathrm{Var}(x_t)}_{\Sigma_x}B^\top+\underbrace{\Omega}_{\text{demand shocks}}. \]

Notereducaed form

Here “reduced form” = moment relations implied by the structural regression

\[ y_t=\alpha + Bx_t+\varepsilon_t,\quad \mathbb E[\varepsilon_t]=0,\ \mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,x_t)=0. \]

You eliminate unobservables by taking expectations/covariances, getting equations in observable moments \((\mu_x,\Sigma_x,\mu_y,\Sigma_y,\Sigma_{yx})\) plus parameters \((\alpha,B,\Omega)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \mu_y &= \mathbb E[y_t] & \text{def}\\ &= \alpha + B\,\mathbb E[x_t] & \mathbb E[\varepsilon_t]=0\\ &= \alpha + B\,\mu_x & \text{notation} \\[6pt] \Sigma_{yx} &= \mathrm{Cov}(y_t,x_t) & \text{def}\\ &= \mathrm{Cov}(\alpha+Bx_t+\varepsilon_t,\ x_t) & \text{substitute}\\ &= B\,\mathrm{Cov}(x_t,x_t) + \cancel{\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,x_t)} & \mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,x_t)=0\\ &= B\,\Sigma_x & \text{notation} \\[6pt] \Sigma_y &= \mathrm{Var}(y_t) & \text{def}\\ &= \mathrm{Var}(Bx_t+\varepsilon_t) & \alpha\ \text{is constant}\\ &= B\,\Sigma_x\,B^\top + \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_t)}_{\Omega} & \text{independence} \end{aligned} \]

Two key consequences:

  • If \(\Sigma_x\) is nonsingular, the cross-moment pins down \(B\):

\[ B=\Sigma_{yx}\,\Sigma_x^{-1}. \]

  • If prices barely move, \(\Sigma_x\approx 0\Rightarrow \Sigma_{yx}\approx 0\) and \(\Sigma_y\approx\Omega\). Then \(B\) drops out of the reduced form—i.e., elasticities aren’t identified from \((\mu_y,\Sigma_y)\). Numerical fixes (e.g., Cholesky on \(\Omega\)) don’t change this information content.

This is the sense in which I wrote the “reduced form” \(\Sigma_y=B\Sigma_xB^\top+\Omega\): it’s just the variance decomposition implied by the structural equation, used to see what the data can (and cannot) identify.

If prices change rarely, \(\Sigma_x\approx 0\Rightarrow \Sigma_y\approx \Omega\), so \(\Sigma_y\) carries (almost) no information about \(B\). Even with a nice Cholesky on \(\Omega\), \(B\) and \(\Omega\) are not separately identified from \((\mu_y,\Sigma_y)\). You need exogenous price variation or instruments. (Consensus view; e.g., Slutsky/identification in demand systems; DLM texts.) [Prado–Ferreira–West 2023; Deaton–Muellbauer 1980]

What to do instead (most effective, given infrequent price moves):

  1. TVP multivariate regression (NDLM) with event weighting

\[ \begin{aligned} y_t&=\alpha_t + B_t x_t+\varepsilon_t,\ \ \varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\Omega),\\ \mathrm{vec}(B_t)&=\mathrm{vec}(B_{t-1})+\eta_t,\ \ \eta_t\sim\mathcal N(0,W), \end{aligned} \]

  • Put a Cholesky parameterization on \(\Omega=L L^\top\) for stability.
  • Use discounting or shrinkage priors so \(B_t\) only updates meaningfully on price-change dates (downweight other \(t\)).
  • Encode micro constraints on compensated elasticities: let \(K_t\) be the Slutsky/compensated matrix; impose symmetry & negativity via \(K_t=-R_tR_t^\top\). Map to Marshallian \(B_t\) using shares/income elasticity when needed. (See Deaton–Muellbauer; West & Harrison/Prado–Ferreira–West.)
  1. (S)VAR / local projections with identified price shocks
  • Stack \((\Delta x_t,\Delta y_t)\) and identify price shocks (VAT changes, cost shifters, policy) or use an external instrument.
  • Compute impulse responses of \(y\) to price shocks; translate contemporaneous/long-run responses to elasticities. This leverages the few price moves without requiring persistent variation.
  1. Exploit cross-section or panels
  • If you can add stores/regions/time-of-day, pool in a hierarchical model to create effective price variation while keeping strong priors on complements (off-diagonals \(<0\)).

Practical spec (compact):

  • Observation: \(y_t=\alpha_t+B_t x_t+\varepsilon_t,\ \varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,LL^\top)\).
  • State: \(\mathrm{vec}(B_t)=\mathrm{vec}(B_{t-1})+\eta_t,\ \eta_t\sim\mathcal N(0,W)\).
  • Priors: horseshoe/ridge on \(\Delta B_t\); for complements, prior mass on \(B_{ij}<0\ (i\neq j)\).
  • Constraints (optional): parameterize compensated \(K_t=-R_tR_t^\top\); map to \(B_t\) using expenditure shares to satisfy Slutsky symmetry/negativity.

Bottom line:

  • Cholesky on \(\Omega\) is good numerically, but covariance of \(Q\) does not recover \(B\) when \(\Sigma_x\) is near-singular.
  • Use a time-varying multivariate regression (NDLM) + event weighting or SVAR with identified price shocks; optionally impose demand-theory constraints to stabilize estimates.
  • If you can add even weak instruments or a small panel, identification improves dramatically.

Refs (consensus sources): Deaton & Muellbauer (1980) Economics and Consumer Behavior; Prado, Ferreira & West (2023) Time Series (TVP/DLM treatment); West & Harrison (1997/2e) Bayesian Forecasting and Dynamic Models (state-space discounting); Kilian & Lütkepohl (2017) Structural VARs (for identification).

# ShinyLive app: estimate self/cross elasticities from within-day price cells (Full, Discount, Loyalty, BOGO)
from __future__ import annotations
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import altair as alt
from shiny import App, Inputs, Outputs, Session, reactive, render, ui

# ---------------- helpers ----------------

def make_B(n: int, own: float = -1.2, cross: float = -0.3, structure: str = "complements") -> np.ndarray:
    """Build n×n elasticity matrix B."""
    B = np.zeros((n, n), float)
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(n):
            if i == j:
                B[i, j] = own
            else:
                if structure == "complements":
                    B[i, j] = -abs(cross)
                elif structure == "subs":
                    B[i, j] = abs(cross)
                else:
                    B[i, j] = 0.0
    return B

def simulate(
    n: int = 3, T: int = 90, seed: int = 0,
    own: float = -1.2, cross: float = -0.3, structure: str = "complements",
    base_price: float = 10.0,
    disc_pct: float = 0.30, disc_prob: float = 0.35,        # “30% off today” cell
    loyal_pct: float = 0.20, loyal_prob: float = 0.30,      # “20% loyalty” cell
    bogo_prob: float = 0.10,                                # “buy 2 get 1” → 2/3 price cell
    sigma_eps: float = 0.15, sigma_u: float = 0.02,
) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, dict]:
    """
    Generate per-day, per-product multiple price cells:
      - Full (always)
      - Discount (on Bernoulli)
      - Loyalty (on Bernoulli)
      - BOGO (on Bernoulli, eff. unit price 2/3 of Full)
    Demand model (log form): y = alpha_it + B x + eps, with common alpha_it for all cells that day.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    B = make_B(n, own, cross, structure)

    alpha_it = rng.normal(0.0, 0.25, size=(T, n))           # day×product demand shock
    u_tj = rng.normal(0.0, sigma_u, size=(T, n))            # small price noise for Full
    eps = rng.normal(0.0, sigma_eps, size=(T, n, 4))        # idiosyncratic noise per cell

    disc_on  = rng.binomial(1, disc_prob,  size=(T, n)).astype(bool)
    loyal_on = rng.binomial(1, loyal_prob, size=(T, n)).astype(bool)
    bogo_on  = rng.binomial(1, bogo_prob,  size=(T, n)).astype(bool)

    rows = []
    for t in range(T):
        pF = base_price * np.exp(u_tj[t, :])
        xF = np.log(pF)

        # Prepare other cells
        pD = pF * (1.0 - disc_pct)
        pL = pF * (1.0 - loyal_pct)
        pB = pF * (2.0 / 3.0)

        xD, xL, xB = np.log(pD), np.log(pL), np.log(pB)
        yF = alpha_it[t, :] + (B @ xF) + eps[t, :, 0]
        yD = alpha_it[t, :] + (B @ xD) + eps[t, :, 1]
        yL = alpha_it[t, :] + (B @ xL) + eps[t, :, 2]
        yB = alpha_it[t, :] + (B @ xB) + eps[t, :, 3]

        for j in range(n):
            # Full (always)
            rows.append({"t": t, "product": f"P{j+1}", "cell": "Full", "price": pF[j], "log_p": xF[j], "log_q": yF[j], "q": float(np.exp(yF[j]))})
            # Discount (if on)
            if disc_on[t, j]:
                rows.append({"t": t, "product": f"P{j+1}", "cell": "Disc", "price": pD[j], "log_p": xD[j], "log_q": yD[j], "q": float(np.exp(yD[j]))})
            # Loyalty (if on)
            if loyal_on[t, j]:
                rows.append({"t": t, "product": f"P{j+1}", "cell": "Loyalty", "price": pL[j], "log_p": xL[j], "log_q": yL[j], "q": float(np.exp(yL[j]))})
            # BOGO (if on)
            if bogo_on[t, j]:
                rows.append({"t": t, "product": f"P{j+1}", "cell": "BOGO", "price": pB[j], "log_p": xB[j], "log_q": yB[j], "q": float(np.exp(yB[j]))})

    df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    meta = {"B_true": B, "n": n, "T": T}
    return df, meta

def within_day_diff(df: pd.DataFrame, n: int, baseline: str = "max") -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """
    General within-day differencing for K cells.
    For each day t and product i:
      - Pick baseline cell = product i's highest price (baseline="max") or lowest price ("min").
      - Pick treated cell  = product i's lowest price if baseline="max" (and vice versa).
      - Build one row with Δlog q_i and Δlog p_j for all products j that day.
      - If product j lacks ≥2 distinct price cells, set Δlog p_j=0 for that j.
    Returns (dmat, X) to match callers (X is same as the Δlog p columns; harmless duplicative).
    """
    if df.empty:
        empty = pd.DataFrame()
        return empty, empty
    dfx = df.copy()
    dfx["price"] = pd.to_numeric(dfx["price"], errors="coerce")
    dfx["log_p"] = pd.to_numeric(dfx["log_p"], errors="coerce")
    dfx["log_q"] = pd.to_numeric(dfx["log_q"], errors="coerce")

    rows = []
    for t, gday in dfx.groupby("t"):
        # keep distinct price levels per product
        gp = {prod: gi.dropna(subset=["price","log_p","log_q"]).drop_duplicates(subset=["price"]).sort_values("price")
              for prod, gi in gday.groupby("product")}
        # products with ≥ 2 distinct price cells
        valid = {prod: gi for prod, gi in gp.items() if len(gi) >= 2}
        if not valid:
            continue

        # precompute Δlog p_j for all j using extreme cells that day (baseline vs treated)
        dlogp_map = {}
        for prod_j, gj in gp.items():
            if len(gj) >= 2:
                if baseline == "min":
                    base_row, trt_row = gj.iloc[0], gj.iloc[-1]
                else:
                    base_row, trt_row = gj.iloc[-1], gj.iloc[0]
                dlogp_map[prod_j] = float(trt_row["log_p"] - base_row["log_p"])
            else:
                dlogp_map[prod_j] = 0.0

        # now build a row for each outcome i that has ≥ 2 cells
        for prod_i, gi in valid.items():
            if baseline == "min":
                base_row_i, trt_row_i = gi.iloc[0], gi.iloc[-1]
            else:
                base_row_i, trt_row_i = gi.iloc[-1], gi.iloc[0]
            dlogq_i = float(trt_row_i["log_q"] - base_row_i["log_q"])

            row = {"t": t, "i": prod_i, "dlogq": dlogq_i}
            for j in range(n):
                pj = f"P{j+1}"
                row[f"dlogp_{pj}"] = dlogp_map.get(pj, 0.0)
            rows.append(row)

    dmat = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    X = dmat.filter(regex=r"^dlogp_")
    return dmat, X

def estimate_B(dmat: pd.DataFrame, n: int, ridge: float = 1e-8) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """OLS (tiny ridge) per row i: Δlog q_i = Σ_j B_ij Δlog p_j + u."""
    if dmat.empty:
        return pd.DataFrame(columns=["row", "col", "B_hat"])
    cols = [c for c in dmat.columns if c.startswith("dlogp_")]
    prods = [c.replace("dlogp_", "") for c in cols]
    out = []
    for i, gi in dmat.groupby("i"):
        y = gi["dlogq"].to_numpy(float)
        X = gi[cols].to_numpy(float)
        XtX = X.T @ X + ridge * np.eye(X.shape[1])
        Xty = X.T @ y
        beta = np.linalg.solve(XtX, Xty)
        for j, b in zip(prods, beta):
            out.append({"row": i, "col": j, "B_hat": float(b)})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

def to_matrix(df_est: pd.DataFrame, n: int) -> pd.DataFrame:
    prods = [f"P{k+1}" for k in range(n)]
    mat = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=prods, columns=prods)
    for _, r in df_est.iterrows():
        mat.loc[r["row"], r["col"]] = r["B_hat"]
    return mat.reset_index().rename(columns={"index": "row"})

# ---------------- Server ----------------

def server(input: Inputs, output: Outputs, session: Session):
    @reactive.calc
    def sim():
        return simulate(
            n=int(input.n()), T=int(input.T()), seed=int(input.seed()),
            own=float(input.own()), cross=float(input.cross()),
            structure=input.structure(), base_price=float(input.base_price()),
            disc_pct=float(input.disc_pct()), disc_prob=float(input.disc_prob()),
            loyal_pct=float(input.loyal_pct()), loyal_prob=float(input.loyal_prob()),
            bogo_prob=float(input.bogo_prob()),
            sigma_eps=float(input.sigma_eps()), sigma_u=float(input.sigma_u()),
        )

    @reactive.calc
    def df():
        df, _ = sim()
        return df

    @reactive.calc
    def meta():
        _, meta = sim()
        return meta

    @reactive.effect
    @reactive.event(input.resim)
    def _resim():
        _ = sim()

    @reactive.calc
    def dmat():
        dmat, _ = within_day_diff(df(), int(input.n()), baseline=input.baseline())
        return dmat

    @reactive.calc
    def est():
        return estimate_B(dmat(), int(input.n()))

    @output
    @render.data_frame
    def B_true_tbl():
        B = meta()["B_true"]
        prods = [f"P{k+1}" for k in range(B.shape[0])]
        return pd.DataFrame(B, index=prods, columns=prods).reset_index().rename(columns={"index": "row"})

    @output
    @render.data_frame
    def B_hat_tbl():
        est_df = est()
        if est_df.empty:
            return pd.DataFrame(columns=["row", "P1"])
        return to_matrix(est_df, int(input.n()))

    @output
    @render.text
    def status():
        est_df = est()
        if est_df.empty:
            return "Estimation pending: need days/products with ≥2 distinct price cells."
        k_min = est_df.groupby("row").size().min()
        return f"Rows estimated: {len(est_df.row.unique())} | examples per row (min): {k_min}"

    @output
    @render.plot
    def scatter():
        import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
        dm = dmat()
        if dm.empty:
            fig = plt.figure(); plt.text(0.5, 0.5, "No Δ yet", ha="center"); return fig
        i = input.row_i(); j = input.col_j()
        col = f"dlogp_{j}"
        if col not in dm.columns:
            fig = plt.figure(); plt.text(0.5, 0.5, "Select another j", ha="center"); return fig
        dmi = dm[dm["i"] == i]
        x = dmi[col].to_numpy(float); y = dmi["dlogq"].to_numpy(float)
        fig = plt.figure()
        plt.scatter(x, y, alpha=0.6)
        if len(x) >= 2 and np.any(x != 0):
            beta = np.polyfit(x, y, 1)[0]
            xs = np.linspace(x.min(), x.max(), 100); ys = beta * xs
            plt.plot(xs, ys); plt.title(f"{i} vs {j}: slope≈{beta:.3f}")
        plt.xlabel(f"Δ log p ({j})"); plt.ylabel(f"Δ log q ({i})")
        return fig

    @output
    @render.ui
    def chart():
        df_long = df()
        prods = [f"P{k+1}" for k in range(int(input.n()))]
        session.send_input_message("row_i", {"choices": {p: p for p in prods}})
        session.send_input_message("col_j", {"choices": {p: p for p in prods}})
        session.send_input_message("plot_prod", {"choices": {p: p for p in prods}})

        prod = input.plot_prod()
        sub = df_long[df_long["product"] == prod].copy()
        sub["cell_disp"] = sub["cell"].astype(str)
        base = alt.Chart(sub).properties(width=650, height=280)
        p1 = base.mark_line().encode(x="t:Q", y=alt.Y("price:Q", title="Price"), color="cell_disp:N")
        p2 = base.mark_line().encode(x="t:Q", y=alt.Y("q:Q",     title="Quantity"), color="cell_disp:N")
        return ui.div(ui.HTML(p1.to_html()), ui.HTML(p2.to_html()))

    @output
    @render.download(filename="simulated_cells.csv")
    def dl_csv():
        return df().to_csv(index=False)

app = App(app_ui, server)
